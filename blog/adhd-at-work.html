ADHD at Work: The Systems That Save Me

8 min read Mims Sivrieva

I was diagnosed with ADHD as an adult, mid-career. It explained a lot. Here's what I've learned about building structures that actually work with a neurodivergent brain — in engineering and beyond.

The myth of just trying harder

For years I assumed everyone's brain worked roughly the same way, and that the people who seemed to have it together were simply applying more effort or discipline than me. Getting diagnosed was the moment that framing collapsed.

ADHD isn't a deficit of trying — it's a difference in how the brain regulates attention, working memory, and executive function. That distinction matters, because it changes the intervention. You don't fix it by trying harder. You fix it by building better systems.

The goal isn't to become neurotypical. It's to build an environment where your brain's actual strengths — hyperfocus, pattern recognition, lateral thinking — get to show up.

Systems I actually use

These aren't productivity hacks from a listicle. They're things I've tested over years, that survived contact with real work:

  • Externalising everything — if it's not written down, it doesn't exist for me
  • Time blocking with hard stops — not "work on X until done", but "work on X until 3pm"
  • Body doubling for deep work — working alongside someone else, even on video
  • Reducing decision load at the start of the day — the fewer choices before 10am, the better
  • Reframing "incomplete" tasks — done-enough is a real category
